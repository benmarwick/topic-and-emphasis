A sample is the part of a population that a researcher actually observes. Because a sample is smaller than the population, any number calculated from it will differ somewhat from the true value. This difference is called sampling error. Sampling error shrinks as the sample grows, but it never disappears completely. Researchers therefore report a range, known as a confidence interval, within which the true value probably lies. A narrow interval indicates a precise estimate, whereas a wide interval indicates that more observations are needed before firm conclusions can be drawn.
