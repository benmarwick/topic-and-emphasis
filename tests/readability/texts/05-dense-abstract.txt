Contemporary operationalisations of institutional legitimacy conflate procedural, distributive and performance-based evaluations, thereby obscuring heterogeneity in the mechanisms through which legitimacy judgements are constituted. Drawing on a multilevel structural equation framework, we disaggregate these evaluative dimensions and estimate their differential contributions across administrative jurisdictions characterised by varying degrees of fiscal decentralisation. Results indicate that procedural evaluations exert disproportionately strong effects in decentralised jurisdictions, whereas performance-based evaluations predominate where administrative responsibility is centralised. These findings problematise unidimensional measurement strategies and underscore the necessity of theoretically differentiated instrumentation in comparative research on institutional legitimacy.
